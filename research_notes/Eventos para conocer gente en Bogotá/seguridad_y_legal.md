## 1. Crime linked to meeting strangers through apps in Colombia (Bogotá and Medellín): official advisories, the 2025–2026 trend, and whether group meetups in public venues are safer

### Takeaway
Dating-app lures followed by drugging and robbery are a documented and current crime pattern (2023 → 2026). The drugs are scopolamine ("burundanga"), benzodiazepines and even veterinary sedatives. The pattern is strongest in Medellín but is also active in Bogotá. Victims are mostly men, especially foreigners and users of gay apps. US, UK, Canadian and Australian advisories are still in force in 2026. Overall theft in Bogotá is falling in 2026, but Medellín's drugging robberies rose sharply. **No study or statistic was found showing that group meetups in public venues are safer than one-on-one dates.** The US Embassy stresses that criminals *first* meet victims in public places (bars, restaurants, hotels), so "public venue" alone does not protect anyone.

**Group vs. one-on-one**
- No empirical source was found that compares crime rates at group meetups with one-on-one dates, in Colombia or elsewhere.
- The one quantified figure from a group-format operator is company-reported: Timeleft says 99% of its experiences happen without a safety incident. There is no one-on-one baseline to compare it with (see Q3). — [Timeleft blog](https://timeleft.com/blog/safety-starts-before-the-dinner/)

### Inferences
- **The documented crime works through a romantic or sexual pretext plus isolation.** Contact is made on the app, the first meeting is in a public bar or restaurant, the victim is drugged, and then they are moved to a hotel or apartment or have their phone and bank accounts drained. A friendship format of 4–6 people with no romantic framing removes the pretext and the isolation at first contact. That plausibly reduces exposure to this specific pattern, but it is **not proven**.
- **The risk moves to "after the event".** Examples: one attendee invites another to "keep going" at a second bar or an apartment, or offers a drink or cigarette. A group format also does not stop drink spiking inside the venue, and a criminal ring could register two or three accomplices into the same table.
- **The target audience matters a lot.** Foreigners, expats and digital nomads (especially men) and users of gay dating apps are the main documented targets. A service marketed to newcomers and foreigners in Bogotá recruits exactly that group.
- **Absolute numbers are small but under-reported.** Bogotá's figures (53 toxic-substance thefts; "37" dating-app cases or arrests in 2025) are small next to the city's overall crime volume. They are probably heavily under-reported: victims feel shame, and foreign victims leave the country.
- **Reputational risk is high.** A single serious incident (a drugging or death) tied to a brand would land on an existing media and embassy narrative.

### Gaps
- No study was found comparing the safety of group meetups with one-on-one dates.
- No Bogotá data was found on crimes linked to meetup or group-event apps as opposed to dating apps.
- The Bogotá "37" figure is ambiguous (thefts vs. arrests).
- No Bogotá dating-app-specific figure for 2026 was found.

## 2. Risks specific to group social events: harassment, drink spiking, scams, discrimination, conflicts between attendees, risks for women, and women-only formats

### Takeaway
In a small-group, public-venue format the main residual risks are:
- drink or cigarette spiking in the Chapinero, Teusaquillo and Suba nightlife zones;
- sexual harassment and unwanted romantic pursuit, mostly affecting women;
- fake or duplicate profiles used to infiltrate groups;
- post-event one-on-one follow-ups, where the dating-app crime pattern can resume.

Bogotá has institutional resources an operator can plug into: the "Pregunta por Ángela" bar protocol, the Línea Púrpura hotline and the Patrulla Púrpura. Women-only formats exist in the market. Whether they are legal under Colombia's anti-discrimination law (Ley 1482 de 2011) was not found to have been tested, so it needs a legal opinion.

- **Venue choice matters more than "public".** Prefer venues whose staff know the "Pregunta por Ángela" protocol. Prefer early-evening or daytime slots and lower-alcohol formats (bookstores, cultural events, coffee). Consider avoiding late-night bars in the Zona Rosa, Chapinero and Suba hotspots, or at least brief attendees on drink safety: never leave a drink unattended, and don't accept drinks or cigarettes from strangers.

  - no sharing of attendees' phone numbers by the operator;

  - an easy post-event report channel;

Because art. 134A requires an "arbitrary" restriction, a documented safety-based rationale likely lowers risk. This is not tested, so a lawyer should confirm. A mixed-gender default that gives women the option of women-only groups, or a minimum number of women per table, is a common market approach, but this was not verified for specific competitors.

## 4. Colombian legal obligations: Ley 1581 (habeas data), RNBD, Ley 1480 (consumer statute), retracto, reversión del pago, platform liability, terms and conditions

### Takeaway
Data protection is the heaviest compliance area. Selfie, liveness and face-matching checks are **biometric data, which is sensitive data**, and they need explicit, optional, informed consent. So may sexual orientation and some personality-test answers. The SIC is an active enforcer: it closed Worldcoin's Colombian operation in 2025–2026, and fines reach up to 2,000 SMMLV and can be imposed on individuals as well as companies.

A small SAS does **not** have to register in the RNBD (the threshold is more than 100,000 UVT in assets), but all other Ley 1581 duties apply, as does the SIC's 2024 guidance on AI and personal data. Under Ley 1480:
- *retracto* (5 business days) generally does not apply once a service has started with the consumer's agreement;
- *reversión del pago* can apply to online card payments (fraud, non-delivery, not as described);
- "portal de contacto" duties are mainly identification and information duties.

Liability for harm at a third-party venue would be decided under general civil liability, mostly fault-based, and depends on how the service is structured. All of this needs a lawyer's confirmation.

### Cited Findings

**Ley 1581 de 2012 (habeas data)**
- Art. 5 defines sensitive data as data affecting intimacy or whose misuse can cause discrimination. It includes racial or ethnic origin, political orientation, religious or philosophical convictions, union or social-organisation membership, **health, sex life and biometric data**. Art. 6 prohibits processing sensitive data unless the data subject gives **explicit** authorization, except in cases set by law. — [Ley 1581 de 2012 (Función Pública)](https://www.funcionpublica.gov.co/eva/gestornormativo/norma.php?i=49981); [Art. 5 (leyonline)](https://leyonline.co/laws/ley-1581-de-2012/articulo-5o-datos-sensibles)
- Art. 23 sanctions: the SIC can impose **personal and institutional fines up to 2,000 SMMLV**. Fines can repeat while the breach continues, and processing activities can be suspended for up to 6 months. — [Ley 1581 de 2012 (Función Pública)](https://www.funcionpublica.gov.co/eva/gestornormativo/norma.php?i=49981)
- **Photos:** the SIC issued a guide on photographs as personal data. Photos that identify a person are personal data. The face carries biometric information, and biometric data is sensitive data under art. 6, requiring prior, express, informed and qualified authorization (Decreto 1377 de 2013 art. 6). — [SIC – Guía fotografías](https://sedeelectronica.sic.gov.co/noticias/sic-expide-guia-para-el-correcto-tratamiento-de-las-fotografias-como-datos-personales); [Externado – derecho de imagen y datos](https://propintel.uexternado.edu.co/derecho-de-imagen-en-la-ley-de-proteccion-de-datos-personales/); [SIC concept Rad. 18-171259](https://www.sic.gov.co/sites/default/files/normatividad/082018/Rad180171259TratamientoDatosSensibles.pdf)
- **SIC precedent on alternatives to biometrics:** the SIC ordered a residential complex to offer entry methods that don't use biometric data and to delete residents' biometric data when they ask. — [SIC communiqué](https://sedeelectronica.sic.gov.co/comunicado/la-superintendencia-de-industria-y-comercio-ordena-un-conjunto-residencial-habilitar-mecanismos-de-ingreso-que-no-impliquen-el-tratamiento)
- **Enforcement example (biometrics):**
  - The SIC ordered the "immediate and definitive closure" of World Foundation and Tools for Humanity's (Worldcoin) personal-data operations in Colombia by Resolución 78798 of **2025-10-03**. The companies had paid people to scan their irises.
  - It confirmed the closure by Resolución 45710 of **2026-06-18** and ordered deletion of all sensitive data collected, including iris codes.
  - Sources: [SIC – sanción](https://sedeelectronica.sic.gov.co/comunicado/la-sic-sanciona-las-sociedades-world-foundation-y-tools-humanity-corporation-con-el-cierre-inmediato-y-definitivo-de-su-operacion-de); [SIC – confirmación](https://sedeelectronica.sic.gov.co/comunicado/la-sic-confirma-la-sancion-de-cierre-inmediato-y-definitivo-de-la-operacion-de-tratamiento-de-datos-sensibles-adelantada-por-world); [El Colombiano](https://www.elcolombiano.com/negocios/sic-cierre-definitivo-worldcoin-colombia-escaneo-iris-AG38586240); [Mobile Time, 2026-07-07](https://mobiletime.la/noticias/07/07/2026/cierre-worldcoin-foundation/)
- **RNBD (Registro Nacional de Bases de Datos):** since Decreto 090 of 2018-01-18, only companies and non-profits with **total assets above 100,000 UVT**, plus public entities, must register. Micro and small enterprises and natural persons are not obliged. — [SIC – RNBD](https://www.sic.gov.co/registro-nacional-de-bases-de-datos); [SIC – Gobierno reduce universo de obligados](https://www.sic.gov.co/gobierno-nacional-reduce-universo-de-obligados-a-cumplir-el-registro-de-bases-de-datos-ante-superintendencia-de-industria-y-comercio); [SIC concept](https://sedeelectronica.sic.gov.co/publicaciones/boletin-juridico/concepto/cuales-personas-estan-obligadas-realizar-el-registro-de-bases-de-datos-personales-en-el-rnbd)
- **AI and personal data:** SIC Circular Externa 002 of **2024-08-21** sets guidelines for processing personal data in AI systems:
  - suitability (*idoneidad*) and necessity: no less invasive alternative should exist;
  - privacy by design;
  - identification, classification and mitigation of risks;
  - data quality: truthful, complete, accurate, updated, verifiable and understandable.
  - Sources: [SIC – Circular 002 de 2024](https://sedeelectronica.sic.gov.co/transparencia/normativa/circular-externa-2-de-2024-de-la-superintendencia-de-industria-y-comercio-lineamientos-sobre-el-tratamiento-de-datos); [PDF](https://sedeelectronica.sic.gov.co/sites/default/files/normativa/Circular%20Externa%20No.%20002%20del%2021%20de%20agosto%20de%202024.pdf); [Dentons Cárdenas & Cárdenas](https://dentons.cardenas-cardenas.com/es/insights/articles/2024/september/10/new-external-circular-provides-guidelines-on-the-processing-of-personal-data-in-ai-systems); [Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/latam/es/services/legal/perspectives/lineamientos-para-tratamiento-datos-en-sistemas-ia.html)
- **International transfers:** SIC Circular Externa 005 of 2017-08-10 lists about 36 countries with an adequate level of protection, **including the United States** (Colombia was the first country to declare the US adequate), as well as Spain, the UK, Mexico, Peru, Germany and France. — [Holland & Knight (2017)](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2017/08/cambios-en-la-transferencia-de-datos-personales-a); [Circular 005 de 2017 text](https://www.alcaldiabogota.gov.co/sisjur/normas/Norma1.jsp?i=70498&dt=S)
- **Marketing contact hours (Ley 2300 de 2023, "Dejen de fregar"):**
  - Advertising messages by SMS, **app or web messaging**, email and advertising calls may only be sent Monday–Friday 7:00–19:00 and Saturday 8:00–15:00, never on Sundays or holidays.
  - The SIC sanctions breaches.
  - The law's stated scope centres on entities supervised by the Superintendencia Financiera and on debt collectors. Whether the advertising-hours rule binds any business is a point for a lawyer; the SIC issued Circular 001 de 2024 on it.
  - Sources: [Ley 2300 de 2023 (Función Pública)](https://www.funcionpublica.gov.co/eva/gestornormativo/norma.php?i=213990); [Data Law SAS FAQ](https://datalawsas.com/ley-2300-colombia-derechos-consumidores); [SIC Circular 1 de 2024](https://normograma.mintic.gov.co/mintic/compilacion/docs/circular_superindustria_0001_2024.htm)

**Ley 1480 de 2011 (Estatuto del Consumidor)**
- **Retracto (art. 47):** applies to distance and non-traditional sales, including e-commerce. The consumer has **5 business days** from the contract (for services) to withdraw. It does **not** apply to "contracts for provision of services whose provision has begun with the agreement of the consumer". — [Ley 1480 (Función Pública)](https://www.funcionpublica.gov.co/eva/gestornormativo/norma.php?i=44306); [Art. 47 (leyes.co)](https://leyes.co/el_estatuto_del_consumidor/47.htm); [Ámbito Jurídico – precisiones retracto](https://www.ambitojuridico.com/noticias/blog-juridico/precisiones-sobre-el-derecho-de-retracto)
- **Reversión del pago (art. 51; Decreto 587 de 2016):**
  - Grounds: fraud; an operation the consumer did not request; product not received on time; product not as described; defective product.
  - The consumer must complain within **5 business days** of learning of it, and the payment participants must reverse within **15 business days**.
  - If a final judicial or administrative decision finds the reversal was not justified, the consumer bears the costs.
  - Sources: [Decreto 587 de 2016 (MinCIT)](https://www.mincit.gov.co/ministerio/normograma-sig/procesos-de-apoyo/gestion-juridica/decretos/decreto-587-de-2016.aspx); [Función Pública](https://www.funcionpublica.gov.co/eva/gestornormativo/norma.php?i=69037); [Ámbito Jurídico](https://www.ambitojuridico.com/noticias/columnista-online/mercantil-propiedad-intelectual-y-arbitraje/el-decreto-de-reversion-de)
- **"Portales de contacto" (art. 53):** a platform where third parties offer products and consumers contact them **must require all offerors to provide information that identifies them**. Academic analysis notes the law leaves unclear how far such portals are liable when they profit from the transaction, since arts. 49, 50 and 53 don't clearly separate portals from e-commerce providers. The SIC issued a 2025 concept on portal obligations (not read). — [Ley 1480 (MinCIT)](https://mincit.gov.co/ministerio/normograma-sig/procesos-misionales/facilitacion-del-comercio-y-defensa-comercial/leyes/ley-1480-de-2011.aspx); [Redalyc – Portales de contacto y su responsabilidad](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/5857/585777333014/html/); [SIC concept 2025 (PDF)](https://www.sic.gov.co/sites/default/files/concepto-boletin-juridico/20257928.pdf)
- **General civil liability:** art. 2341 of the Código Civil requires damage, fault (intent or negligence) and a causal link. Art. 2356 covers dangerous activities under a risk-based, stricter regime (analysed in CSJ SC2111-2021). — [Redalyc – responsabilidad por actividades peligrosas (CSJ-SC2111 de 2021)](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/4175/417571103014/html/)

### Inferences
- **Classify the data up front.** Likely data held:
  - identity and contact: name, phone/WhatsApp, email;
  - profile: photo, age and gender, personality-test answers;
  - dietary restrictions (can be health data);
  - possibly sexual orientation or relationship status;
  - activity: attendance, ratings, misconduct reports and bans;
  - verification: selfie, liveness and ID images.

  Sensitive under art. 5 are the biometric data, sex-life or orientation data, and health data. Personality-test answers only become sensitive if they reveal religious, philosophical or political views or mental health. Keep sensitive fields **optional**, collect a separate explicit consent, minimize them, and set short retention periods. For example, keep only a verification result or hash, not the raw selfie or ID. SIC precedent suggests offering a non-biometric alternative.
- **RNBD registration very likely does not apply** to a start-up SAS. 100,000 UVT is roughly COP 5,000 million at 2025's UVT of about COP 49,799, which is background and unverified. But the policy, consent, security and rights duties do apply, and the SIC has shown it will act fast against biometric collection.
- **US vendors.** Using US-based LLM or KYC vendors is a transfer or transmission to an "adequate" country under Circular 005/2017. Data transmission agreements with processors are still needed (background, unverified).
- **Provider vs. portal.** An operator that designs the format, sets the price, picks the venue and matches the people probably acts as a **provider** of its own service, not just a "portal de contacto". It would then carry direct Ley 1480 duties (information, quality, *idoneidad*) for the matching and event-organisation service. The venue remains responsible for its premises, food and drink.
  - Liability for a crime committed by a third party at the venue would most likely be judged by fault. Did the operator act negligently, for example by ignoring prior reports about a banned user, failing to verify people, or choosing a venue known for incidents? Documented diligence (verification, bans, venue vetting, incident logs) is therefore the main legal shield.
  - Whether courts would read an implied contractual "obligación de seguridad" into the operator's service is for a lawyer to confirm.
- **Refunds.** Retracto likely applies when a ticket is bought online days before the event, if the consumer withdraws within 5 business days and before the event starts. It does not apply once the event has happened. Refund policies should honour this rather than say "no refunds". Reversión del pago risk (chargebacks for "service not as described") argues for clear, precise event descriptions.
- **T&C and disclaimers.** Clauses in the terms and conditions cannot waive statutory consumer rights (background, unverified). Useful elements:
  - 18+ requirement and truthful identity;
  - code of conduct and zero tolerance for harassment;
  - the enforcement ladder;
  - a statement that the venue is an independent provider;
  - personal-safety guidance;
  - an incident-reporting channel;
  - the right to exclude users;
  - a data-processing policy link and AI-use disclosure.

### Gaps
**Needs lawyer confirmation (background, unverified in this session):**
- **Ley 1581 procedure:**
  - Consultas are answered within 10 business days (extendable by 5), and reclamos within 15 business days (extendable by 8) under Ley 1581 arts. 14–15.
  - A written "Política de Tratamiento de la Información" with the minimum content set by Decreto 1377 de 2013 (compiled in Decreto 1074 de 2015) is required.
  - Proof of authorization must be kept.
  - Security incidents must be reported to the SIC (reportedly within 15 business days via the RNBD platform, even by companies not required to register databases).

  - Art. 47 lists other exceptions, and refunds must be paid within 30 calendar days.
  - Art. 53 requires a registry of offerors' name, ID, address and phone for complaints.
  - Art. 50 sets e-commerce information duties.
  - Arts. 42–43 cover abusive clauses.
  - Art. 61 sets the maximum SIC consumer fine at 2,000 SMMLV.
- **Other questions not researched:**
  - Whether Ley 1493 de 2011 / PULEP registration for "espectáculos públicos de las artes escénicas" could apply if events include performances. Probably not for dinners, but check for cultural events.
  - Whether the operator's ban and misconduct lists raise due-process or rectification issues under habeas data.

## 5. Running the business with an AI agent: company form (SAS), RUT/DIAN, electronic invoicing, payment-gateway KYC, whether an AI can own, represent or be liable for a business, and Colombian AI policy

### Takeaway
In Colombia an AI agent cannot be a shareholder, legal representative or liable party:
- an SAS must be formed by natural or legal persons, and its legal representative must be a natural or legal person;
- DIAN, RUT, the Cámara de Comercio and payment gateways (e.g., Wompi) need a human legal representative's identity documents.

All civil, consumer and data-protection liability, including SIC fines that can be imposed on individuals, falls on the company and its human administrators. The AI is legally a tool. There is no AI statute in force as of September 2026:
- **CONPES 4144** (February 2025) is non-binding policy.
- The government's AI bill (**PL 043/2025 Senado – 324/2025 Cámara**) was archived in mid-2026 and substantially re-filed as **PL 025/2026**.
- The operative AI rule touching customer data is **SIC Circular 002/2024**.

### Inferences
- **Practical structure.** A human founder (or a holding company controlled by humans) is the SAS's sole shareholder and legal representative. That person signs the Cámara de Comercio, RUT/DIAN, bank and gateway KYC, and the e-invoicing provider contract. The AI agent operates under documented internal mandates, such as refund limits, ban criteria and escalation rules.
- **Who is liable in practice.** The SAS is liable, as both data controller (*responsable del tratamiento*) and consumer provider. Its human legal representative and administrators can face personal fines from the SIC under Ley 1581 art. 23 and possible administrators' liability (Ley 222 de 1995 art. 24; background, unverified). If a harmful decision was made "by the AI", liability does not shift to the AI or, in most cases, to the AI vendor. It stays with the operator.
- **Tasks a human should own even if the AI runs the day-to-day:**
  - safety incidents: suspected drugging, sexual harassment or assault, police reports and Fiscalía or Police data requests;
  - bans in disputed cases;
  - habeas data complaints and SIC requirements;
  - chargeback and reversión disputes;
  - venue relationships after an incident.
- **Transparency about AI.** Telling users they are interacting with an AI agent, and giving them a way to reach a human, fits CONPES 4144's rights and ethics principles and SIC Circular 002/2024. It is probably required if the AI bill is ever enacted, since transparency obligations are typical of such bills, but that is unverified. It lowers regulatory and reputational risk even though no statute in force was found to require it.

### Gaps
- Administrators' personal liability under Ley 222 de 1995 art. 24 and the Ley 1258 regime was not verified.
- No Colombian rule currently in force was found that requires disclosing chatbot or AI use to consumers.
- The transparency provisions of PL 025/2026 were not verified.
- The status of PL 025/2026 is contradictory across sources.
- It was not verified whether "Ley 2502 de 2025" (reportedly adding penalties for AI-enabled impersonation) exists and is relevant.
- An Ámbito Jurídico headline, "Congreso aprueba proyecto de ley de datos para impulsar la inteligencia artificial", appeared in results, but its date and content were not verified. — [Ámbito Jurídico](https://www.ambitojuridico.com/noticias/tecnologia/defensa-nacional-y-seguridad-privada/congreso-aprueba-proyecto-de-ley-de-datos)
- These details were not researched:
  - RUT issuance steps and the pre-RUT at the Cámara de Comercio;
  - electronic-invoicing handling of small B2C ticket sales (e.g., the POS electronic document);
  - onboarding requirements of other gateways (PayU, Mercado Pago, ePayco), including SARLAFT and AML questionnaires.
