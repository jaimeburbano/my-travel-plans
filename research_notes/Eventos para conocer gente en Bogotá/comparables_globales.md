## 1. Timeleft: product, formats, pricing (LatAm/Colombia), revenue/funding, scale, retention, city-launch playbook, restaurant economics

### Takeaway
Timeleft leads the category. A personality quiz matches about 6 strangers to a Wednesday dinner at a partner restaurant, sometimes followed by a bar meetup for all of that night's diners. Revenue comes almost entirely from consumers: roughly a €20/month subscription or a single ticket, with food paid separately to the restaurant. I found no evidence that restaurants pay Timeleft. Reported traction was about €18M ARR and about 150K monthly participants in 200+ cities by Aug 2025, on only about $9M raised and about 100 staff. Timeleft opened cities with paid ads rather than ambassadors, peaked at about 325 cities, then cut back to about 220 with a depth threshold of 50+ weekly participants per city. It already runs in Bogotá, Medellín and Cali. In 2026 it is adding drinks, coffee and run formats.

### Cited Findings

**How the product works**
- At signup users take a personality questionnaire. The algorithm groups about 6 compatible strangers ("you + 5"), picks the restaurant based on the users' budgets, and provides icebreaker games and topics. — [Primicias (Ecuador)](https://www.primicias.ec/noticias/entretenimiento/cenas-desconocidos-quito-guayaquil-timeleft-app/) (attribution via search summary)
- Details are revealed in stages. First the time, then the venue a few hours before, then partial group composition such as industries and zodiac signs. Minutes before the start, an in-app question game unlocks for when conversation stalls. — [Yahoo Finanzas, "Probamos la app Timeleft"](https://es-us.finanzas.yahoo.com/noticias/probamos-app-timeleft-promete-cena-025600310.html); [Ohlalá (Argentina, Jun-2024)](https://www.somosohlala.com/lifestyle/gastronomia/timeleft-la-aplicacion-que-te-lleva-a-cenar-con-5-desconocidos-como-funciona-nid10062024)

All dinners happen only on Wednesdays." — [Timeleft blog, "Dinner with strangers"](https://timeleft.com/blog/dinner-with-strangers/) (attribution via search summary)
- After dinner there is an "after-party" at a bar where all of that day's participants can meet. Colombian users describe this on social media. — [TikTok "Timeleft Bogotá"](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/timeleft-bogota) (low-reliability source)

**How it opens a city, and staffing**
- Timeleft did not start by hiring city managers or ambassadors. It "opened one city per day for a year using nothing but ads", peaking at 325 cities. People "didn't need hand-holding or icebreaker cards; they simply showed up." Hyper-expansion "came with costs", leading to a cutback to about 220 cities. The refined playbook is to "open fast with ads, but double down on depth (50+ weekly participants) before sustaining long-term operations." — [Tim Frin Substack](https://timfrin.substack.com/p/inside-timelefts-journey-to-connecting) (attribution via search summary)
- Each city now has a City Manager. Restaurants "can drop a line on WhatsApp and start a conversation with the City Manager of their city." — [Timeleft, "The Ultimate Guide to Timeleft Restaurants"](https://timeleft.com/post/the-ultimate-guide-to-timeleft-restaurants/)

- Content and SEO are also used for acquisition, for example city guides like "How to Make Friends in NYC (2026)" and "Timeleft vs Meetup". — [Timeleft blog](https://timeleft.com/blog/how-to-make-friends-in-nyc/); [Timeleft vs Meetup](https://timeleft.com/blog/timeleft-vs-meetup/)

**Restaurants: selection and economics**
- Only restaurants rated at least 4.3 on Google Reviews "and approved by a crowd of gourmet enthusiasts" are featured. Timeleft also looks for venues that "vibrate online", share their passion on Instagram and have an engaged community. — [Timeleft restaurants guide](https://timeleft.com/post/the-ultimate-guide-to-timeleft-restaurants/) (attribution via search summary)
- Diners pay the restaurant directly. A 2026 review says Timeleft "has no part in that transaction". — [Introvrs](https://www.introvrs.com/blog/timeleft-alternatives) (attribution via search summary). No source says restaurants pay Timeleft a fee or commission.

**Safety and group composition**
- Timeleft says it screens members and venues in advance and moderates group chats in real time. Every dinner is in a public venue. No personal details are shared to book. There is a rule against pursuing someone after they say no. It claims "99% of experiences happen without a safety incident" and says "a report button is a last resort, not a strategy." — [Timeleft safety blog](https://timeleft.com/blog/safety-starts-before-the-dinner/)
- Timeleft aims for a balanced gender mix, but women "often make up over 60% of participants". The algorithm "pulls from a different slice of the city each week". — [Timeleft blog](https://timeleft.com/blog/dinner-with-strangers/) (attribution via search summary)

**Latin America and Colombia**

### Inferences
- **Revenue per active participant.** €18M ARR is about €1.5M per month. Divided by 150K monthly participants, that is about €10 per participant per month. This fits a mix of €20 monthly subscriptions, discounted multi-month plans, single tickets, and much lower LatAm/Asia prices.
- **Attendance frequency.** 6,500 dinners/week × about 6 seats is about 39K seats per week, or about 170K seats per month. That is close to 150K monthly participants, so the typical participant attends about once a month, not weekly.
- **Churn signal.** "3M+ guests" (cumulative) against 150K monthly participants means monthly actives are about 5% of everyone who has ever attended. That suggests heavy trial-and-leave. Caveat: "guests" may count attendances rather than unique people.
- **Staffing.** About 100 staff across 200+ cities is about 0.5 staff per city. Operations are centralized and automated, restaurants provide the hospitality, and there is no paid host at the table. Revenue per employee is roughly €180K.
- **Market mix.** 45% of revenue from the US is about €8M ARR there. LatAm is probably a small share, given prices of roughly US$8 per dinner or MXN 450 per month. Treat LatAm consumer price points as about 40–60% of US/EU levels.
- **Restaurant value.** Restaurants apparently do not pay. What they get is guaranteed Wednesday covers (midweek traffic) and exposure. That is the same value a Bogotá venue would get from a group-attendance service.
- **Competitive overlap for Bogotá.** Timeleft is the incumbent for "dinner with strangers" in Bogotá. A service that sends matched groups to third-party events would be adjacent rather than head-to-head. However, Timeleft's move into drinks, coffee and run formats shows it is widening beyond dinner.

### Gaps

## 2. Nomad Table (nomadtable): product, cities, pricing, model, traction, team, how events are sourced

### Takeaway
"Nomad Table" appears to be **nomadtable** (nomadtable.app). It is a bootstrapped, solo-founder app, software only, for solo travelers to find others nearby and join or create user-generated activities such as dinners, sightseeing and coffee. It does not run events itself. It earns money through a "Nomadtable Plus" subscription at $6.99/week, $14.99/month or $69.99/year. Reported traction grew from 75K MAU and $18K MRR within about 6 months (Aug-2025 podcast) to later claims of 1–2M users and $65K–$140K MRR, or "$2M+ ARR". I found no Bogotá- or Medellín-specific data.

### Cited Findings
- It is a location-based social app that connects solo travelers in real time for shared activities: dinner companions, walking tours, coffee meetups with digital nomads, and connecting with travelers visiting the same destination on overlapping dates. — [nomadtable, Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=jayraavi.SoloMatch&hl=en_US); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/nomadtable-travel-friends/id6736921913); [nomadtable.app](https://nomadtable.app/)
- Features:
  - Join or create activities
  - Add your next destination and get matched into a group chat with travelers visiting at the same time
  - AI-powered activity and venue suggestions
  - In-app messaging and group chats
  - Source: [MWM app listing](https://mwm.ai/apps/nomadtable-travel-friends/6736921913); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/nomadtable-travel-friends/id6736921913)
- Nomadtable Plus costs $6.99 per week, $14.99 per month or $69.99 per year. It unlocks the full list of nearby travelers instead of a limited selection, profile boosts to the top of the nearby list, and all AI recommendations. — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6736921913) (attribution via search summary)
- The founder, Jay Raavi, "bootstrapped from idea to 75,000 monthly active users and $18,000 MRR in under six months". The episode also covers "ghost marketing" to validate the idea before building, and three MVP pivots. — Podcast "May I Have Your Retention Please?", 15-Aug-2025: [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/kw/podcast/nomadtable-the-solo-travel-meetup-app-that-scaled-to/id1825694010?i=1000722141291); [Podscan](https://www.podscan.fm/podcasts/may-i-have-your-retention-please/episodes/nomadtable-the-solo-travel-meetup-app-that-scaled-to-75k-maus-and-18k-mrr-in-6-months)
- "1M downloads & $65K/month as a solo developer." — [Medium (Yuma Ueno)](https://medium.com/@yumaueno/how-jay-raavi-built-nomadtable-to-1m-downloads-65k-month-as-a-solo-developer-1ad81dc6d411)
- "$140K MRR and 1.8M users in 15 months while traveling." — [Jay Raavi, Intro.co profile](https://intro.co/JayRaavi) (attribution via search summary)
- "2M+ users and $2M+ ARR, 100% bootstrapped." — [Jay Raavi on X](https://x.com/jayvraavi) (attribution via search summary). The website and app listing say "1M+ users and counting". — [nomadtable.app](https://nomadtable.app/)
- Growth relied on UGC short-video marketing. — [The Superwall Podcast, "Copy This UGC Strategy, It'll Make Your App Go Viral"](https://open.spotify.com/episode/5VSmAAlnKCvgmB7rQVtksJ)

### Inferences
- Early monetization was about $18K MRR from 75K MAU, or roughly $0.24 per MAU per month. That is typical of a freemium paywall on discovery. Revenue scales with downloads and paywall conversion, not with operations.
- The model needs essentially no event operations. Users create activities; the company only supplies discovery, chat and a paywall. In Bogotá it would mostly serve foreign travelers and nomads, not locals, which is a different audience from a local-events matching service.
- The user figures (1M, 1.8M, 2M+) and revenue figures ($65K/month, $140K MRR, $2M+ ARR) come from different dates and definitions (downloads vs. users). Treat them as a growth trajectory, not one audited number.

### Gaps
- No city-level figures for Bogotá or Medellín, and no evidence of Nomadtable-run events in Colombia.
- Team size beyond the solo founder is unknown.
- Retention and churn are unknown, although the founder has discussed retention on a retention-focused podcast.
- If the coordinator meant a different "Nomad Table", such as a dinner series for digital nomads in Medellín, I found no such company.

## 3. Other comparables with real data: 222, Pie, Meetup, Bumble BFF/Geneva, InterNations, Mundo Lingo, Gringo Tuesdays, Sofar Sounds, Couchsurfing Hangouts, LatAm Timeleft-like clones

### Takeaway
The comparables show four revenue models:
1. **Consumers pay a curation fee or subscription:** Timeleft, 222 ($22.22/month), InterNations Albatross (about US$12/month), Nomadtable Plus.
2. **Venues pay per head:** 222 charges $3 per diner sent to restaurants and $5 per member sent to other experiences. Mundo Lingo charges only the bar and is free for attendees.
3. **Organizers pay a SaaS fee:** Meetup, from $55 per group per month for Pro, raised in 2024.
4. **Ticket revenue split with artists:** Sofar, with $15–30 tickets and a 63/37 net split.

Heavy reliance on volunteers created legal risk (Sofar's $460K settlement) or scaled slowly (InterNations ambassadors). Big-tech friendship products have struggled to monetize: Geneva had no revenue before being folded into Bumble BFF.

- **Partial reversal:** In Sep-2025 Meetup launched "Meetup Starter", its "first-ever free plan for new organizers", and reported +20% year-over-year growth in new registrations. — [Business Wire, 30-Sep-2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250930663024/en)

**Bumble BFF and Geneva**
- In Feb-2024 Bumble moved to revive BFF "in wake of dating app downturn". — [TechCrunch, 28-Feb-2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/28/bumble-looks-to-revive-its-friendship-focused-bff-product-in-wake-of-dating-app-downturn/)
- On 18-Sep-2025 Bumble relaunched BFF around a Groups tab: discover communities, join chat rooms, plan hangouts and track events in a calendar. Group discovery was not due until Feb-2026. Geneva was shut down and its members moved to BFF. Bumble had told investors that **Geneva had generated no revenue as of 30-Jun-2025**. Bumble research found 47% of young adults want more friends to do activities with. — [TechCrunch, 18-Sep-2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/18/bumble-bffs-revamped-app-is-here-focusing-on-friend-groups-and-community-building)

**InterNations (paid expat community events)**
- 5.7M members in 420 cities, with about 4,000 events and activities per month. — [Relocate Magazine (2025)](https://www.relocatemagazine.com/the-best-worst-countries-for-living-abroad-internations-0925); [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=org.internations&hl=en_US)

**Mundo Lingo (language-exchange bar nights)**

A Bogotá edition is listed at 7:30pm–12am. — [Couchsurfing event, Mundo Lingo Bogotá](https://www.couchsurfing.com/events/mundo-lingo-bogota). Montreal coverage from 2024: [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/montreal/multilingual-bar-night-mundo-lingo-plateau-1.7170486)

**Sofar Sounds (secret gigs, originally run by volunteer ambassadors)**
- Sofar raised $38.1M over four rounds, including a $25M Series C in May-2019 led by Battery Ventures and Union Square Ventures, with Octopus Ventures and Virgin Group. — [Variety, 2019](https://variety.com/2019/biz/news/whats-next-for-sofar-sounds-and-its-genius-volunteer-based-business-model-1203325982/); [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/articles/business/8512661/sofar-sounds-raises-25-million-from-investors)

### Inferences
- **The venue-pays model is proven but small per head.** 222 charges $3–5 per person delivered. At 100,000+ members sent, that is roughly $300K–500K in cumulative venue revenue, a meaningful secondary stream but not a standalone business. A Bogotá model could charge venues per attendee delivered, a flat fee (Mundo Lingo), or a consumption minimum, while consumers pay a curation fee.
- **Free for attendees, paid by the venue, works for recurring, bar-anchored formats.** Mundo Lingo and Gringo Tuesdays rely on bar consumption and door timing ("free before 10pm"). They scale to tens of cities, not hundreds, and depend on a local organizer.
- **Consumer subscriptions work when the product is a recurring ritual.** Timeleft has a weekly Wednesday dinner. 222 has a monthly subscription. InterNations charges about US$12/month for event discounts. At LatAm price points, the ritual matters more than the one-off event.
- **Charge only after density.** InterNations waited for 60–80 stable attendees before charging for events. Timeleft needed 50+ weekly participants per city. Both suggest paid products need proven local liquidity first.
- **Events apps drift toward communities.** Pie and Bumble BFF both moved from events or matching to ongoing groups and communities. The likely reason is to keep users engaged between events and after they have found friends.

### Gaps
- 222: retention, no-show rates, share of revenue from venues vs. consumers, and team size.
- Pie: the MAU conflict (130K vs. 50K+).
- InterNations: Bogotá community size and event prices.
- Mundo Lingo: how much bars pay (flat vs. per head) and the current city count after 2020.
- Gringo Tuesdays: founder, current cities and bar economics.
- Couchsurfing Hangouts: launch and current status. There is no primary source on whether Hangouts sits behind the paywall.
- Confra: traction.
- Other Timeleft clones in Colombia specifically were not found. The separate Bogotá-market researcher may cover them.

## 4. Products that match strangers to attend EXISTING third-party events together (concerts, bar events, workshops, talks)

### Takeaway
Two kinds of product exist in this niche:
- **Small, mostly free social-discovery apps** built around existing events: WashedUp (LA), Radiate, Muse, CrowdMate, EventBuddie, Beatmatch and AYSE. They use event group chats, "who's going" lists and crews. None has disclosed meaningful revenue. Radiate, the largest dedicated concert-buddy app, has about 260K downloads, and its visible monetization is a ticket marketplace.
- **222's model**, which is closest to the Bogotá concept. 222 sends curated, AI-matched groups to partner venues' existing experiences, such as dinners and comedy shows. Consumers pay a curation fee or subscription, and venues pay $3–5 per head.

At the venue, people recognize each other mainly through pre-event group chats, reserved tables, and, in language exchanges, flag stickers or tables organized by language. I found no documented use of wristbands or pins by these apps.

### Cited Findings
- **222:** Matched users are invited to "curated in-person experiences at local venues", including dinners, comedy shows and other group activities. — [Online Personals Watch, Mar-2026](https://onlinepersonalswatch.com/2026/03/222-uses-ai-to-match-strangers-at-real-life-events/). Partner venues pay "$3 per person sent to a restaurant" and "$5 per member sent for other experience partnerships". 222 handles marketing, and 100,000+ members have been sent to partners. — [222 partners](https://partners.222.place/)
- **WashedUp (LA):**
  - Launched in Jan-2026. Free. Organized as a public benefit corporation.
  - Users "post or join any activity like a comedy show, a hike, dinner, or a beach day". A group of 3–8 forms, and a private group chat opens.
  - Plans can set headcount, age range or women-only.
  - "No more flakes": group chats open only after people commit.
  - Browsable feed of local events.
  - Solo founder Liz Bridges taught herself to code in Dec-2025 and shipped six weeks later.
  - Sources: [WashedUp About](https://washedup.app/about); [WashedUp](https://washedup.app/); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/washedup-meet-people-in-la/id6759820053); [TechCrunch roundup, Apr-2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/05/as-people-look-for-ways-to-make-new-friends-here-are-the-apps-promising-to-help/)
- **Radiate:** Described as "the biggest dedicated concert buddy app at around 260,000 downloads". It started in EDM and festival culture before expanding to broader nightlife. It offers event-specific group chats, lets users browse who's going, and has a 3D social map and a **ticket marketplace**. — [ConcertBuddy blog (2026)](https://www.concertbuddy.net/en/blogs/best-apps-to-find-people-to-go-to-concerts-with) (competitor-authored); [Radiate on App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/radiate/id939284774)
- **Muse:** "Each event carries a group chat and a live count of who's going, including who's going solo". Users can join or start a crew for the night. — [Muse blog](https://findmuse.app/blog/music-life/how-to-find-people-to-go-to-concerts-with)
- **CrowdMate:** "Mark interested, see who's in, and buy tickets together". — [CrowdMate](https://crowdmate.app/)
- **EventBuddie:** Match with people going to the same concerts and plan a meetup. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/eventbuddie/id6748877517)
- **Beatmatch** matches on Spotify and Apple Music listening. **AYSE** covers student concert outings and ticket buying and selling. — [ConcertBuddy blog](https://www.concertbuddy.net/en/blogs/best-apps-to-find-people-to-go-to-concerts-with); [AYSE App Store](https://apps.apple.com/np/app/ayse/id6563147388)
- **Pie:** AI quiz → groups of six → group chat before the event. — [TechCrunch, 4-Mar-2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/04/andy-dunns-new-app-pie-uses-ai-to-help-you-make-friends). By Aug-2026 Pie was moving away from events toward communities. — [TechCrunch, 25-Aug-2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/25/andy-dunns-startup-pie-becomes-less-of-an-events-app-and-more-of-a-social-network/)
- **Nomadtable:** Users create or join activities, and destination-based group chats match travelers with overlapping dates. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/nomadtable-travel-friends/id6736921913)
- **How people recognize each other at the venue:**
  - Mundo Lingo flag stickers show languages spoken. — [Mundo Lingo FAQ](https://mundolingo.org/faq)
  - Gringo Tuesdays organizes groups by language and level, facilitated by native speakers. — [Eventbrite](https://www.eventbrite.com/e/gringo-tuesdays-language-exchange-buenos-aires-tickets-2000410464549?aff=ebdssbdestsearch)
  - Timeleft uses a reserved table, a staged reveal of the group and an in-app question game. — [Yahoo Finanzas](https://es-us.finanzas.yahoo.com/noticias/probamos-app-timeleft-promete-cena-025600310.html)
  - WashedUp, Pie and Radiate coordinate through pre-event group chats. — sources above

### Inferences
- **Most "go together to an existing event" apps have unproven monetization.** Visible models are freemium, ticket marketplaces or buy-together, and venue per-head fees. Only 222 publishes a clear venue price ($3–5 per head), which suggests venue fees are low per attendee. In Bogotá, venue-side revenue per head would likely be a fraction of 222's USD figures, or would need to be structured as a consumption minimum or bar-tab commission.
- **Sending matched groups to other people's events cuts operating cost.** There is no venue sourcing per group and no programming to produce. But it moves the experience risk (quality, crowd) to the event host, and it depends on hosts accepting a matched group and making it visible.
- **Recognition at the venue is an unsolved design space in the documented apps.** Flags and stickers (Mundo Lingo) are cheap, proven and culturally familiar in Bogotá's expat and language scenes. A Bogotá service could adapt them, for example a sticker or pin by group color, plus a reserved table or corner and a pre-event chat.
- **WashedUp's "chat opens only after commit" rule** is a low-cost anti-flake mechanism relevant to no-shows (see section 6).

### Gaps
- No verified data on ticket-affiliate commission rates, group-ticket economics or revenue for Radiate, Muse, CrowdMate, EventBuddie or WashedUp.
- No data on attendance and show-up rates for these apps.
- No source found on apps using wristbands or pins to identify matched strangers at third-party events.
- No evidence found of large ticketing platforms such as Eventbrite, Fever or Dice running a "match me with strangers going solo" feature at scale. This could not be checked further.

## 5. Failures and cautionary tales

### Takeaway
The failures fall into four patterns:
- **Fake or vanity metrics:** IRL claimed 20M users, 95% of them bots, and shut down in 2023.
- **No monetization:** Geneva had zero revenue before Bumble folded it into BFF (2025). Hey! VINA raised only $1.59M, never scaled beyond a niche and shut in late 2024.
- **Monetizing a community too late or too abruptly:** Couchsurfing's 2020 paywall triggered an exodus. Meetup's 2024 organizer price rises drew backlash, followed by a free Starter plan in 2025.
- **Labor risk from volunteer ambassadors:** Sofar's $460K settlement.

Even the winner, Timeleft, cut back from 325 to about 220 cities after ads-driven hyper-expansion.

### Cited Findings
- **IRL:**
  - A board investigation found that 95% of the app's reported 20M users were "automated or from bots". The company shut down on 27-Jun-2023. — [TechCrunch, 26-Jun-2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/06/26/irl-shut-down-fake-users/); [Fortune, 25-Jun-2023](https://fortune.com/2023/06/25/irl-shutting-down-startup-admits-95-percent-of-messaging-app-users-were-fake/)
  - In Jun-2021 IRL had announced 400% growth over 15 months, more than $200M in funding and a $1.17B valuation. — [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/irl-messaging-app-shuts-down-95-of-users-were-bots/454821). Another account cites $170M raised. — [imfounder](https://imfounder.com/entrepreneurship/startup-failures/irl-app-fraud/)
  - It was designed to facilitate in-person meetups through messaging and group plans. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/06/26/irl-shut-down-fake-users/)
  - In 2022 it laid off 25% of staff (about 25 people), and employees doubted the 20M MAU claims. The SEC opened an inquiry in late 2022. The board suspended CEO Abraham Shafi in Apr-2023. The SEC charged Shafi with securities fraud in Jul-2024. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2023/06/25/irl-shutting-down-startup-admits-95-percent-of-messaging-app-users-were-fake/); [imfounder](https://imfounder.com/entrepreneurship/startup-failures/irl-app-fraud/)

- **Geneva (acquired by Bumble):** It had generated no revenue as of 30-Jun-2025. It was shut down in Sep-2025 and its users were migrated into Bumble BFF. — [TechCrunch, 18-Sep-2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/18/bumble-bffs-revamped-app-is-here-focusing-on-friend-groups-and-community-building)
- **Hey! VINA (women's friendship app, launched 2016):** It shut down in late 2024 citing operational and product challenges. It "never scaled beyond a niche audience", raised only about $1.59M, "was unable to compete with Bumble BFF's resources", and had not been updated since Sep-2023. — [Threvi (a competitor's page)](https://threvi.app/vs/hey-vina/); [GoFrendly blog, 26-Dec-2024](https://www.gofrendly.com/blog/2024/12/26/hey-vina-is-shutting-down-the-best-alternatives-for-women-to-make-new-friends/); launch coverage at [TechCrunch, 2016](https://techcrunch.com/2016/01/26/hey-vina-is-a-tinder-for-girl-friends)

Smaller cities "thinned out". — [Fodor's](https://www.fodors.com/news/hotels/is-couchsurfing-still-worth-it-how-the-once-free-travel-community-changed-after-going-corporate); [Trespot](https://trespot.com/couchsurfing-alternatives.html)
- **Meetup's decline:**
  - WeWork paid about $156M in 2017, and AlleyCorp bought it in 2020 "for a fraction" of that. The business was "struggling" even before COVID, with layoffs in Nov-2019. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meetup); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2020/03/30/wework-sells-meetup-investor-group-led-alleycorp/)
  - Under Bending Spoons (2024), organizer prices rose. One organizer reported about $98 rising to about CAD 600. — [Andy Piper](https://andypiper.co.uk/2024/10/18/meetup-com-is-so-over/)
  - This was followed by a free organizer plan in Sep-2025. — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250930663024/en)
- **Sofar Sounds:** Unpaid "ambassadors" ran shows. - **Timeleft's over-expansion:** It peaked at 325 cities, and hyper-expansion "came with costs", leading to a cutback to about 220 cities. — [Tim Frin Substack](https://timfrin.substack.com/p/inside-timelefts-journey-to-connecting) (attribution via search summary)
- **Pivots away from pure events:** Pie became "less of an events app and more of a social network" (Aug-2026). — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/25/andy-dunns-startup-pie-becomes-less-of-an-events-app-and-more-of-a-social-network/). Bumble BFF moved from one-to-one matching to groups and communities (Sep-2025). — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/18/bumble-bffs-revamped-app-is-here-focusing-on-friend-groups-and-community-building)
- **Mundo Lingo:** It went from 30 cities in 2019 to suspended events in 2020, showing how exposed a pure in-person model is to shocks. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mundo_Lingo)

### Inferences
- **Measure attended seats and paid seats, not downloads or MAU.** IRL's collapse and the Pie MAU discrepancy both show that app metrics can overstate real-world activity.
- **The "once you make friends you churn" problem.** I found no source that quantifies it directly. But it is consistent with:
  - Timeleft's roughly 5% monthly-active to cumulative-guest ratio
  - Timeleft deliberately mixing groups each week
  - Pie and Bumble adding persistent communities
  - Timeleft adding formats (drinks, run, coffee) to give users new reasons to return
- **Monetize from day one, but set expectations.** Timeleft charged from the start and grew. Geneva never charged and died. Couchsurfing and Meetup changed pricing abruptly on an existing free or cheap community and suffered backlash.
- **Volunteer-run operations are cheap but carry legal and quality risk.** Sofar is the clearest case. A Bogotá model using unpaid "ambassadors" should check Colombian labor-law exposure. That analysis is outside this researcher's scope.

### Gaps
- No quantitative source on the "make friends then churn" dynamic, such as cohort retention curves for any friendship app.
- No post-mortems found for other in-person friendship startups beyond IRL, Hey! VINA and Geneva, for example Lunchclub or We3. The session's search quota ran out before these could be checked.
- No data on how Meetup's paid-organizer count or revenue changed after the 2024 price rise.

## 6. Recurring operational lessons: cold start and liquidity per city, no-shows, group composition and gender balance, safety, role of a human host

### Takeaway
Liquidity thresholds are concrete. Timeleft sustains a city only at 50+ weekly participants. InterNations charges for events only after 60–80 stable attendees. Paid ads can open a city fast (Timeleft), but depth decides whether it survives. Groups converge on 4–8 people (Timeleft 4–6, Pie 6, WashedUp 3–8). Female-skewed demand (Timeleft often more than 60% women) pushes operators toward women-only formats. Safety relies on public venues, screening and chat moderation. The human role ranges from no host at all (Timeleft relies on restaurant and app mechanics) to volunteer ambassadors (InterNations, early Sofar) to native-speaker facilitators (Gringo Tuesdays). No company publishes no-show rates. The design responses are upfront payment (Timeleft, 222) and commit-before-chat (WashedUp).

### Cited Findings
- **Cold start (Timeleft):** It opened cities with ads alone, one per day for a year, reaching a peak of 325. It then cut back to about 220 with the rule "open fast with ads, but double down on depth (50+ weekly participants) before sustaining". — [Tim Frin Substack](https://timfrin.substack.com/p/inside-timelefts-journey-to-connecting) (attribution via search summary)

### Inferences
- **City-launch rule of thumb from the comparables:** Pre-sell before building anything (Timeleft's Typeform + WhatsApp + Stripe). Concentrate on one city until about 50+ attendees per week, the Timeleft sustain threshold. Introduce venue or consumer charges once recurring attendance is stable, the InterNations 60–80 per event benchmark. For a single-city Bogotá launch, that means one or two fixed weekly slots so demand concentrates, as Timeleft did with Wednesday and Mundo Lingo did with a fixed bar night.
- **No-shows.** Prepayment plus commit-then-reveal mechanics (chat unlocks, staged venue reveal) are the standard tools. Expect to over-book and to manage gender balance actively, given the female skew in demand.
- **Hosting.** A paid host at every event does not appear necessary for dinner-type formats. At a third-party event, though, where the group must find each other in a crowd, a light-touch visible anchor matters: a reserved table, flags or stickers, or a facilitator. The comparables using facilitators (Gringo Tuesdays) or door hosts (Mundo Lingo) are exactly the bar-event formats.
- **Unpaid "ambassador" labor** has proven legally fragile (Sofar). Paid part-time community managers (Timeleft) or free-membership perks for volunteers (InterNations) are the common alternatives.
