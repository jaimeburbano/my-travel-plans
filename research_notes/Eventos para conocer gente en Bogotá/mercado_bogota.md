## 1. Demand: who in Bogotá would want this?

### Takeaway
There are clear signals of latent demand, but none of the data measures it directly in Bogotá. Nationally, 16% of Colombians aged 12+ report loneliness (ENSM 2025), with higher rates among migrants and separated or widowed people. One in five households now has a single person (2025). Bogotá has about 6.2M adults, about 590k Venezuelan migrants and a smaller expat/nomad community (Medellín leads that segment). Dating-app use is established (Tinder had about 560k active Android users in Q4 2025), and press coverage describes growing app fatigue. I found no Bogotá-specific loneliness figure and could not retrieve any Reddit threads.

### Cited Findings
**Loneliness and social isolation (national, 2025–2026)**
- The Encuesta Nacional de Salud Mental 2025 (ENSM 2025) conducted more than 120,000 interviews in all 32 departments. It finds that **16% of Colombians over 12 experience loneliness**. Rates are higher among women, separated or widowed people, the LGBTIQ+ population, **migrants** and victims of the armed conflict. In the Pacific region the figure is **above 20%**. El Espectador's headline calls it the largest mental-health "radiography", showing "un país más ansioso y más solo". — [El Espectador](https://www.elespectador.com/salud/la-mayor-radiografia-de-la-salud-mental-en-colombia-revela-un-pais-mas-ansioso-y-mas-solo/); [Cambio, June 2026](https://cambiocolombia.com/salud-y-bienestar/articulo/2026/6/colombia-y-su-nuevo-espejo-emocional-lo-que-refleja-la-nueva-encuesta-nacional-de-salud-mental)
- Analysis of the ENSM 2025 argues that loneliness "is a social phenomenon, not an individual problem" that "requires collective responses". — [Cambio, June 2026](https://cambiocolombia.com/salud-y-bienestar/articulo/2026/6/colombia-y-su-nuevo-espejo-emocional-lo-que-refleja-la-nueva-encuesta-nacional-de-salud-mental)
- Single-person households rose from **16.8% (2020) to 20.2% (2025)** nationally (DANE, Encuesta de Calidad de Vida 2025). The headline reads "1 de cada 5 hogares ya es unipersonal". — [El Colombiano](https://www.elcolombiano.com/negocios/encuesta-calidad-vida-colombia-2025-dane-vivienda-pobreza-hogares-HH35672353)
- An "unwanted loneliness" (soledad no deseada) bill is on the agenda of the new Congress. This comes from a headline dated 28 June 2026; I could not access bill details. — [Cámara de Representantes – Poder Legislativo](https://poderlegislativo.camara.gov.co/2026/06/28/la-soledad-no-deseada-el-proyecto-que-busca-compania-en-el-nuevo-congreso/)
- Bogotá Cómo Vamos, Encuesta de Percepción Ciudadana 2025, found higher satisfaction, optimism and pride in the city. **Young people are "the most optimistic and participatory"** but perceive more insecurity. **5.6%** of Bogotá residents show moderate or severe anxiety (4.1% moderate, 1.5% severe). No loneliness indicator appeared in the summaries. — [Bogotá Cómo Vamos](https://bogotacomovamos.org/encuesta-de-percepcion-ciudadana-2025/); [Bogota.gov.co](https://bogota.gov.co/mi-ciudad/planeacion/encuesta-de-percepcion-2025-asi-percibe-la-ciudadania-bogota)
- In the Red Cómo Vamos comparative survey 2025, Medellín and Manizales, despite high optimism, have lower shares of "good mental health" than the national average. — [Medellín Cómo Vamos](https://www.medellincomovamos.org/epc-comparada/)

### Inferences
- Applying the national ENSM 16% loneliness rate to Bogotá's 6.24M adults gives roughly **1.0M adults experiencing loneliness**. This is order-of-magnitude only: ENSM covers ages 12+ and Bogotá's own rate was not found.
- Rough size of the **22–40 cohort**: adults aged 18–59 number about 4.98M (6.24M − 1.26M). If spread evenly by age, ages 22–40 would be about 2.25M. The real figure is probably 2.0–2.5M because the 25–39 cohorts are large. This is an estimate, not a DANE figure.
- Venezuelans are about **7.4% of Bogotá's population** (591k / 7.95M), and ENSM flags migrants as higher-loneliness. Their spending capacity was not researched, so they may be a price-sensitive segment.
- The expat/nomad segment in Bogotá exists but is secondary to Medellín (Antioquia 40% vs Bogotá 26% of Colombian search interest). Bogotá's core addressable market is more likely locals: young professionals, people who moved from other regions, and remote workers.
- About 0.5–0.7M monthly users of major dating apps (Android snapshot) combined with a "dating-app fatigue" narrative supports positioning around in-person, non-romantic activities.

### Gaps
- **No Bogotá-specific loneliness or social-isolation figure** was found. The ENSM 2025 regional or Bogotá breakdown and age breakdown could not be accessed. The Bogotá Cómo Vamos 2025 summaries mention anxiety but not loneliness.
- **Reddit (r/Bogota, r/Colombia) threads could not be retrieved.** The search engine returned no Reddit results and the budget ran out, so there is no first-hand forum evidence beyond the Tripadvisor thread.
- The following were not found for lack of search budget:

  - An exact DANE count for ages 22–40.

  - Foreign residents (non-Venezuelan) in Bogotá.

## 2. Event supply: what happens, how it is published and discovered, volume, free vs paid, zones

### Takeaway
Supply is very large, and public supply is largely free. In 2025, Idartes alone ran **about 43,000 activities with 2.2M participants**, including 8 free "Festivales al Parque" with more than 660k attendees. Commercial and community events are spread across Instagram/TikTok "planes" accounts, Luma (tech/AI), Eventbrite (language exchanges, free events), Fever (nightlife, experiences) and Meetup (hobby groups). The social venues identified cluster in the northern corridor (Chapinero – Calle 85/Zona Rosa – Parque El Virrey/93 – Unicentro), with free cultural and comedy supply in La Candelaria. I found no platform-level volume figures for Eventbrite, Luma, TuBoleta, Eticket or Passline.

### Inferences
- About 43,000 Idartes activities a year is roughly **118 public cultural activities per day**, mostly free. A matching service could fill its calendar at zero ticket cost to users, but free events weaken the case for venue-side monetization.
- Discovery is fragmented: tech on Luma, language exchanges and free events on Eventbrite, nightlife on Fever, hobbies on Meetup, and day-to-day plans on IG/TikTok accounts. A curation layer that also solves "who do I go with" has a plausible role.
- The evening social venues identified (language exchange, run clubs, board-game cafés, Zona T nightlife, Luma tech events) sit mostly along the northern corridor: Chapinero, Calle 85/Zona T, El Virrey/Parque 93, Chicó, Unicentro. La Candelaria and Teusaquillo lean toward free cultural events. These are qualitative observations, not counts.

### Gaps
- No event-volume figures were found for Eventbrite, Luma, Meetup, **TuBoleta, Eticket, Passline** or Fever in Bogotá. There is no measured free vs paid share for commercial events, and no count of bookstore or company events.
- There is no quantified breakdown of venues or events by zone (Zona G, Parque 93, Usaquén, Teusaquillo). The Secretaría de Cultura/Idartes agenda platform and its event count were not retrieved.

## 3. Existing local alternatives and competitors

### Takeaway
**Timeleft is the direct competitor and has run in Bogotá since 19 June 2024.** It offers weekly Wednesday dinners with about 5 strangers and an afterparty. Price reports put it at about USD 8 per dinner plus memberships, with users paying their own food. Around it sit many free or low-cost substitutes:
- Gringo Tuesdays, the language exchange and party that has run for more than 10 years at Calle 85: free before 6pm, COP 10k–20k later.
- Meetup groups (the largest "make friends" group has about 1.6K members).
- Social run clubs, board-game cafés and communities.
- Bogotá Nomads (free monthly expat dinners).

I found no Colombian startup with this exact concept. Nomad Table and Mundo Lingo were not confirmed in Bogotá.

## 5. Pricing norms and willingness to pay

### Takeaway
The 2026 minimum wage is **COP 1,750,905** (+23%), or **COP 2,000,000** with transport aid. The decree was provisionally suspended but re-issued transitorily at the same value. Local price anchors for social outings are low:
- Free public festivals.
- Language-exchange covers of **COP 0–20,000**.
- Timeleft at about **USD 8 (≈COP 32,000)** plus food.
- Festival-goers spending about **COP 29,000** each.
- Older Asobares data showing the largest group of bar-goers spending **COP 15,000–25,000 per night**, but about a third spending **COP 75,000–100,000**.

### Cited Findings
- **Minimum wage 2026:** — [Buk](https://www.buk.co/blog/salario-minimo-colombia); [Holland & Knight, Dec 2025](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/12/colombia-decreta-aumento-del-salario-minimo-y-auxilio-de-transporte)
  - **COP 1,750,905/month**, up 23% (+COP 327,405) from COP 1,423,500 in 2025.
  - Transport aid is **COP 249,095**, bringing the total to **COP 2,000,000**.
  - The daily wage is COP 58,363.50.
  - The ordinary hourly wage was COP 7,959 until 14 July and is **COP 8,338 from 15 July 2026**, when the work week dropped to 42 hours.
  - Originally set by Decrees 1469 and 1470 of 29 Dec 2025.
- **Legal status of the decree:**
  - The Consejo de Estado provisionally suspended the decree in Feb 2026. — [Holland & Knight, Feb 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/02/suspension-provisional-del-decreto-que-fijo-el-salario-minimo)

  - **Gringo Tuesdays:** free until 6pm, COP 10,000 from 6–8pm, COP 20,000 after 8pm. — [Colture](https://colture.co/bogota/work-learn-volunteer-bogota/learn-spanish-in-bogota/gringo-tuesdays-language-exchange-and-party-in-bogota/)

### Inferences
- **Timeleft-equivalent fee:** at about COP 32,000 (approximate conversion), a dinner fee is about **1.8% of the monthly minimum wage** and about **55% of a minimum-wage day**, before food. For professionals earning several times the minimum, it is a small ticket.
- **Festival spend:** COP 19,000M ÷ 660K ≈ **COP 28,800 per festival attendee**. This is a rough baseline for incidental spend at a free cultural outing.
- **Plausible fee range:** the anchors (free public events, COP 10–20K covers, about COP 32K for Timeleft) point to a per-event matching fee of roughly **COP 15,000–35,000**, or a monthly plan priced near Timeleft's, for a mass-market Bogotá offer. This is inference and should be tested.
- **Inflation adjustment:** the Asobares spend bands would be materially higher in 2026 COP. Minimum-wage growth from 2018 to 2026 is large (to COP 1.75M), so the old bands understate current spending.

### Gaps
- Typical income of the 22–40 professional segment in Bogotá was not found: DANE GEIH median labour income, graduate salaries (Observatorio Laboral) and remote-worker incomes.
- Current 2026 prices were not collected for workshops (ceramics, cooking), stand-up comedy, concerts, Fever experiences (e.g., Candlelight) and club covers beyond one COP 20K example.
- There is no survey on willingness to pay for social-matching services in Colombia.
- Timeleft's actual COP price was not confirmed.

## 6. Payment and communication rails

### Takeaway
Colombia's payment rails are well suited to low-ticket consumer payments:
- **Bre-B** (Banco de la República instant payments, fully live since 6 Oct 2025) had **about 34.9M users and 105M keys by May 2026**, handles up to 5M operations a day, is free for person-to-person transfers, and now supports recurring debits.
- **Nequi** has **more than 26M registered users**.
- Card and PSE gateways charge about **2.65–3.49% plus COP 700–900 plus 19% VAT on the fee**, which is steep on tickets around COP 30K.
- **WhatsApp** is effectively universal, used by about 92% of internet users.

## 7. Venue side: incentives of bars, restaurants, bookstores and organizers; the relacionista público (RP) model

### Takeaway
Three models coexist in Bogotá, but none of the sources shows venues paying commissions to platforms:
- **Free entry, venue earns on drinks:** Mundo Lingo-style exchanges and board-game groups.
- **Staggered cover that fills an early or weeknight slot:** Gringo Tuesdays, running on Tuesdays for more than 10 years.
- **User pays the platform and pays the venue separately:** Timeleft's Wednesday dinners and afterparty.

Weeknight-anchored formats (Timeleft on Wednesdays, TNC Night Run on Wednesdays, Gringo Tuesdays) suggest venues value filling slow nights. I found no Bogotá-specific figures for RP commissions or slow-night occupancy. Spanish benchmarks suggest about 20% of the entry price or a per-head fee.

### Cited Findings
- **Timeleft's venue model:** "they handle the venue, the group, and the time… **you'll pay for your food and drinks at the venue**." Dinners run on **Wednesdays**, followed by an afterparty at a bar. — [Timeleft](https://timeleft.com/); [TikTok – Timeleft Bogotá](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/timeleft-bogota)
- **Gringo Tuesdays** runs a **Tuesday** language exchange with a staggered cover (free → COP 10K → COP 20K) at Vintrash Bar on Calle 85. - **RP (relaciones públicas / relacionista público) model, Spain proxy only, not Bogotá** — [Premium Guest](https://www.premiumguest.com/es-blog/que-es-un-a-relaciones-publicas-de-una-discoteca); [Madrid Lux](https://madridlux.com/es/p/Explicacion-Trabajo-de-relaciones-publicas-en-Madrid); [You Barcelona](https://youbarcelona.com/es/p/comunidad_rrpps-youbarcelona):
  - RPs are paid on commission, "usually around **20% of the final entry price**".
  - Examples: **€1.50 per ticket** and **€2 per VIP person**, or tiered pay per night (10 people = €40, 15 = €60, 30 = €75, 40+ = €90).
  - RPs manage guest lists ("listas"), often open lists.
  - An RP is described as the business's "image": young, well-connected, paid on commission. — [Infobae México (CDMX "RP de antro", Apr 2024)](https://www.infobae.com/mexico/2024/04/17/cuanto-gana-un-rp-de-antro-este-es-el-sueldo-en-cdmx-y-otras-ciudades-del-pais/)

### Inferences
- The Bogotá venues and formats observed anchor on **Tuesdays and Wednesdays**, which fits the premise that venues want to fill slow nights. A group-matching service delivering guaranteed headcounts on Monday–Wednesday is the most credible pitch. Plausible asks: a free welcome drink, a reserved table, a group discount, or a revenue share on consumption.
- If Bogotá's RP economy resembles the Spanish or Mexican model (commission per head or a percentage of cover or consumption), venues are already used to paying for delivered customers. That would give a precedent for a per-attendee referral fee. This is **unverified for Bogotá**.
- Timeleft appears to earn from users, not venues, based on the sources found. A competitor could differentiate with a two-sided model: lower user fees subsidized by venue payments.

  - Group-discount norms.

  - Asobares 2025–2026 figures.

## 8. Marketing costs (Meta/TikTok ads, micro-influencers)

### Takeaway
Colombia is one of the cheapest paid-social markets in the region:
- **Meta:** CPM about **USD 2** (US about USD 16, Mexico about USD 3.9) and median CPC about **USD 0.22**, which spiked to about USD 0.86 in July 2026.
- **TikTok:** reported CPMs range from about **COP 1,500–4,000 to USD 2–12**. The sources conflict.
- **Micro-influencer posts:** about **COP 0.3–3M**.

### Cited Findings
- **Meta CPM:** Colombia's CPM is about **USD 2.00**, vs about USD 16 in the US, USD 3.92 in Mexico and USD 2.44 in Chile. The monthly CPM trough was about **USD 1.70 in May 2026**. — [Superads – CPM Colombia](https://www.superads.ai/facebook-ads-costs/cpm-cost-per-mille/colombia); [AdAmigo – benchmarks by country 2026](https://www.adamigo.ai/blog/meta-ads-cpm-cpc-benchmarks-by-country-2026)
- **Meta CPC:** Colombia's median CPC averaged about **USD 0.22** across the year. The low was **USD 0.12 (Apr 2026)**, with spikes to about **USD 0.30 (Oct 2025)** and about **USD 0.86 (Jul 2026)**. — [Superads – CPC Colombia](https://www.superads.ai/facebook-ads-costs/cpc-cost-per-click/colombia)
- Meta cost-per-lead rose about **21% globally in 2025**. Local conversion rates and creative can outweigh cheap CPMs. — [Fuelads – LatAm Q2 2026 CPL benchmarks](https://fuelads.tech/benchmarks-latam-2026); [Ryze](https://www.get-ryze.ai/blog/meta-ads-cost-benchmarks-by-industry-2026)
- **TikTok, Colombia (ranges vary by source):**
  - CPC **USD 0.02–0.15** and CPM **USD 2–6**.
  - CPM **COP 1,500–4,000**, described as "2–4× cheaper than Meta for audiences under 35".
  - CPM **USD 4–12**, "up to 40% lower than Meta".
  - CPM **USD 3–8**.
  - Recommended minimum budget: **USD 50–100/month**, or **COP 800,000–1,500,000/month** according to another source.
  - Sources: [Consolidación Digital](https://www.consolidaciondigital.com/blog/performance-marketing/tiktok-ads-colombia); [Webhexup](https://www.webhexup.com/cuanto-cuesta-tiktok-ads-colombia/); [Itera](https://somositera.com/cuanto-cuesta-publicidad-tiktok-ads/); [Novoads](https://novoads.ai/recursos/cuanto-cuesta-publicidad-en-tiktok)

  - **COP 300,000–3,000,000 per post** for 10K–100K followers. — [El Tiempo](https://www.eltiempo.com/cultura/gente/cuanto-dinero-gana-un-influencer-en-colombia-segun-numero-de-seguidores-832871); [La FM](https://www.lafm.com.co/entretenimiento/cuanto-dinero-puede-ganar-un-influencer-en-colombia-328036)
  - Alternatively, USD 25–1,250 on TikTok and USD 100–800 on Instagram for 10K–50K followers. — [Blind Creator](https://www.blindcreator.com/blog/costo-influencer-colombia-tarifas-negociacion-presupuesto.html); [Taggbox](https://taggbox.com/es/blog/influencer-pricing/)

### Inferences
- **Budget illustration:** COP 1M a month (≈USD 250 at an approximate rate) buys about **125,000 Meta impressions** at a USD 2 CPM, or about **1,100 clicks** at a USD 0.22 CPC. At an assumed 2–5% click-to-paid conversion, not sourced, that would be about 23–57 paying users, a CAC of about USD 4–11 (≈COP 18–44K). That is on the order of one event fee, so paid acquisition only pays off with repeat use or subscriptions.
- **Influencer vs ads:** one micro-influencer post (COP 0.3–3M) costs about 1–3 months of that ad budget. The small Bogotá "planes" accounts (25–50K followers) are the natural fit, especially on fee + affiliate terms.
- **Seasonality:** because CPCs spiked in Oct 2025 and Jul 2026, launch timing and budgets should allow for volatility.

### Gaps
- No Bogotá-only (city-level) Meta or TikTok benchmarks, no benchmarks for the events or social vertical, and no Colombian CAC benchmark for consumer apps were found.
- Specific quotes from Bogotá "planes" accounts or micro-influencers were not obtained.
